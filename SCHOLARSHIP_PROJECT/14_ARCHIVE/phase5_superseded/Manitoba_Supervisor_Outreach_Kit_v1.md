# University of Manitoba MSc Computer Science (thesis): Supervisor Outreach Kit v1

**Deadline: 1 December 2026** (international, Fall 2027). Verified on umanitoba.ca, 7 Nov 2026 (E1).

## Go / no-go decision (decide by 12 Nov)

Verified rules that make this hard:

- **A professor must agree to supervise you *before* you apply.** "Applications without an endorsement from a faculty member will not be considered" (E1).
- The faculty minimum is **GPA 3.0 (B) over your last two years**, calculated on your university's grading scale (E1). Your Years 4–5 GPA is **2.98** (E0, my calculation). The university must confirm how it treats this.
- **IELTS Academic 6.5** is required (FGPS).
- Funding if admitted: **minimum CAD 20,000 per year for 2 years** (E1). That is less than the study-permit proof of funds (CAD 22,895 + tuition), so a **funding gap** remains.

**Recommendation:** pursue Manitoba **only if** a professor replies positively by about **25 November** **and** you can take IELTS before applying. Otherwise drop it and focus on Sweden, AISS, EDISS and CMU-Africa.

## Step 1: find 5–8 professors (30 minutes)

1. Open the Department of Computer Science faculty list on umanitoba.ca. Note professors (not instructors or adjuncts) working on **distributed systems, cloud computing, networking, security, software engineering, or systems reliability**.
2. For each one, read the two most recent publication titles and note **one specific topic** you can connect to your real work.
3. Email **one professor at a time per research group**. Personalise the first two sentences every time; generic mass emails are ignored.

## Step 3: research interest statement (about 300 words; draft to adapt per professor)

> **Research interests: reliable and secure distributed infrastructure for payment services**
>
> In Ethiopia's growing digital-payments sector, outages and security incidents directly affect merchants and customers. In my work on [⏳ verified role] I operate the infrastructure behind payment services: Linux servers, container platforms, CI/CD pipelines and monitoring tools [⏳ keep only verified tools].
>
> Two problems recur in practice. First, failures are often partial and slow to detect: one dependency degrades, and the symptoms appear elsewhere. Second, configuration and deployment changes are a frequent source of incidents, yet change risk is usually judged by experience rather than evidence.
>
> I would like to study these questions at graduate level:
>
> 1. How can observability data (metrics, logs, traces) be used to detect and localise faults in microservice systems earlier and with fewer false alarms?
> 2. How can deployment pipelines assess the risk of a change before it reaches production?
> 3. How can security controls (identity and access management, network policies) be verified continuously in hybrid cloud environments?
>
> My undergraduate foundation includes Distributed Systems, Computer Security, Network & System Administration, Operating Systems and Compiler Design. I know my mathematics background is weaker; I am [⏳ real preparation step]. I am looking for a supervisor whose group works on [area], so that my research can address problems I have seen in production.

## Step 4: if a professor agrees

- They submit a **supervisor support letter** inside your application (requested as a "recommendation").
- **Fee:** check the application portal (not captured in this session's evidence).
- Apply online through FGPS **by 1 December**, with transcripts, CV, IELTS and references.
- **Funding consideration is automatic** once admitted (E1). The UMGF and department entrance awards have a **27 February** deadline (E1).
