I want to learn [one or two MSIT courses or concentrations from the official curriculum, and why each matters to your work] alongside engineers from across the continent who face the same constraints I do: unreliable connectivity, fast-growing transaction volumes, and the need for security that regulators and customers trust.

## Essay C: Contribution to your community and giving back (about 230 words)

Ethiopia is moving rapidly from cash to digital payments. For a small merchant, a reliable payment service means more customers and safer income. For a family, it means paying school fees without travelling to a bank. When these systems fail, the people with the least margin for error lose the most, and they lose trust in digital money.

I have spent my career on the infrastructure behind these services, and I want to make it trustworthy at a much larger scale. After the MSIT I will return to Ethiopia's digital-finance sector to design payment infrastructure that is secure, reliable and affordable to run.

Giving back also means sharing knowledge. I will [one specific, realistic commitment, e.g. run a free workshop each semester for Computer Science students at Debre Tabor University on Linux, automation and reliability; mentor two junior engineers each year]. Universities outside Addis Ababa rarely have access to industry practice, and I know from my own studies how much that would have helped me.

The Mastercard Foundation invests in young Africans who will lift others as they rise. I want to be that kind of engineer: one who builds the systems ordinary people depend on, and who helps the next generation build them too.
