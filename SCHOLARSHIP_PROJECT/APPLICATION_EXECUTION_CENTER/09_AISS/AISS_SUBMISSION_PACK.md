# 2. Eligibility and scholarship rules (E1, re-read 8 Nov 2026)

- **Admission:**
  - a recognised bachelor's degree plus an academic, professional or project background that supports the study path
  - education documents and transcript are assessed
  - documents count 50% (minimum 35/50); interview 50% (minimum 35/50); programme threshold 70/100
- **Scholarship:**
  - apply for it during admission
  - awarded only to the **10 highest-ranked** applicants
  - **at most 10% per nationality**
  - not combinable with another EU-funded Erasmus Mundus scholarship
  - you must study the full 24 months and meet the minimum mobility rules
  - **€1,400/month**, covering insurance, mobility travel, housing and food; tuition waived
- **English:** IELTS Academic or IELTS Online 6.5 (≥ 5.5 each); TOEFL iBT; PTE Academic 62 (≥ 59 each); Cambridge.
  - **An Ethiopian degree is not exempt.** The exemption covers only studies in the US, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, UK, Switzerland or the EU/EEA.
- **Your position:** eligible to apply. Competitive only through a strong portfolio, video and interview. **Not GPA-independent.**

## 3c. Social impact statement

My aim is to help make those decisions reliable, explainable and fair, by designing them with the merchants and users they affect and by keeping the infrastructure under them efficient enough to run sustainably in low-resource settings. After AISS I will bring this to Ethiopia's digital-finance sector [⏳ concrete plan].

# 7. Interview

**Presentation (3 slides at most, no reading):**

1. **Who I am** (3 min): education, then infrastructure work, then one real incident or rollout story (STAR).
2. **What I want to explore** (4 min): R1 or R2, why it matters in Ethiopia, and what you need to learn (machine learning foundations, participatory methods, sustainability assessment).
3. **What I will do with it** (3 min): career plan in Ethiopia's digital finance.

**Likely questions, with answer outlines:**

| Question | Outline (use your real facts) |
|---|---|
| Why AISS and not a pure computer-science master's? | You need AI + society + design together; your problems are socio-technical |
| Your grades are average. Why should we select you? | Upward trend (final year 3.20); strong A− in demanding courses; years of production responsibility; the portfolio shows execution |
| What is participatory design? | Involving the people affected throughout design. Give an example: merchants co-defining fraud-alert rules |
| Which AI methods do you know? | Be honest: introductory AI course (B); you will build the foundations in year 1. Name what you have read |
| A project that failed? | A real incident: what you changed afterwards |
| How is your work linked to sustainability? | Financial inclusion (SDG 8), resilient infrastructure (SDG 9); energy cost of computing |
| Which mobility track and why? | Choose after reading the track pages; link it to R1 or R2 |
| If you get no scholarship? | Your self-funding plan: honest (section 9) |
| How will you use the degree in Ethiopia? | A specific role and problem, not "help my country" in general |
| Teamwork in a multicultural group? | A real example of working with colleagues or partner banks |
